# A2 · The Sociologist · 05-climate-disaster

## 伪剧情（英文 · 3 pseudos）

### p1
- **fragments**: why-0, how-0, result-1

An exceptionally heavy rainy season, a force indifferent to human settlement, reveals the structural vulnerability of communities positioned downstream. The swollen Euphrates becomes an instrument of exposure, flooding the homes, farms, and schools of Deir Ezzor and Raqqa — the very foundations of subsistence and social reproduction. This is not random misfortune but the materialization of a geographic and economic margin, where populations bear the hydrological costs of an upstream nation's water management and a state's limited capacity for preemptive infrastructure investment.

### p2
- **fragments**: how-1, how-2, how-3

The state's response mobilizes the machinery of sovereignty — the navy deploys boats for evacuation — yet this apparatus arrives as a compensatory measure after the collapse of basic infrastructure, an earthen bridge. The sequence reveals a gap: official warnings are issued, but the material reality they describe outpaces the protective capacity available to the warned. The subsequent negotiation with Turkey, an upstream power, underscores the periphery's lack of control over a vital resource flowing through its territory.

### p3
- **fragments**: result-0, result-1, result-3, result-4, result-5

The result is a stark ledger of loss: drowned children, whose vulnerability was neither mitigated by warning nor by infrastructure, alongside widespread devastation to subsistence economies and public institutions. The president's visit to the flood zone performs a ritual of attention, while the substantive power shift — Turkey's agreement to reduce releases — is negotiated between states, beyond the reach of the affected population. The gap persists between the administration of crisis and the lived reality of those who pay its price.
