## 伪剧情（英文 · 3 pseudos）

### p1
- **fragments**: why-0

The late endorsement from the sitting president, the apex of party power, was deployed as a resource to secure the renomination of a state-level officeholder. This intervention from the center reconfigured a peripheral intra-party contest, granting one candidate a decisive structural advantage and rendering the incumbent's long-standing position untenable. The resource gap—access to the ultimate endorsement—proved insurmountable, transforming a primary election into a demonstration of top-down political capital allocation.

### p2
- **fragments**: how-0, how-1, how-2

The mechanism of control was direct: the endorsement from the party's national executive descended into a state-level primary. This translated into an overwhelming 64% of the vote for the endorsed candidate, a margin that signaled not mere preference but the consolidation of factional power. The defeat of the incumbent senator was the logical consequence of this resource transfer, where the periphery's autonomy was nullified by the center's endorsement capital.

### p3
- **fragments**: result-0, result-1, result-2

The result is a realignment within the ruling party's structure. The first loss of renomination by a sitting Texas senator since 1970 marks the displacement of an established political class by a faction empowered from the national center. The subsequent general election frames a contest between this newly installed party loyalist and a representative from the minority party, where the primary outcome has already determined the structural advantage. The resource gap established in the runoff will define the opposition.
