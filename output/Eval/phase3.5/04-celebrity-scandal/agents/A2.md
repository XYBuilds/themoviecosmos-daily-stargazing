# A2 · The Sociologist · 04-celebrity-scandal

## 伪剧情（英文 · 3 pseudos）

### p1
- **fragments**: why-0

The digital sphere operates as a marketplace where scandal is a commodity. The content creator's alleged fabrication was not an act of malice but a strategic deployment of tools to generate attention, the primary currency of the platform economy. This transforms reputational damage into a transferable asset, where the creator's gain is directly extracted from the celebrity's social and economic capital.

### p2
- **fragments**: how-0, how-1

The apparatus of state power is activated asymmetrically. A warrant is swiftly secured for the peripheral actor—the content creator—while the institutional response to the initial, damaging claims against the celebrity was slower, a delay that permitted the erosion of the celebrity's commercial value. Enforcement thus protects the established celebrity-capital system, but only after significant damage has been absorbed by the individual at its center.

### p3
- **fragments**: result-0, result-1

The consequences fall along a stark resource gap. The celebrity's endorsement income and public presence—concentrated forms of social and financial capital—were immediately halted. The creator, operating from the periphery of institutional legitimacy, faces the punitive power of the state. The system's final action re-establishes a hierarchy: the celebrity's position may be salvageable, but the creator is made an example, reinforcing who truly holds the means of production in the attention economy.
